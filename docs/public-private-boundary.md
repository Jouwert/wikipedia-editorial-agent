# Public / private boundary

Editorial work about living people has a lower publication threshold than ordinary project work.
This document states what is published and what is withheld, and why.

## Principle

The public value here is **the governance model** — mission, neutrality rule, preservation
standard, review authority. The material that must never be public is anything that identifies a
subject, a candidate, a reviewer, or an access route.

Where a design point cannot be described without revealing a person or an access method, it is
described as an obligation rather than a procedure.

## Published

- The editorial mission and the distinction between corrective subject selection and neutral prose.
- The evidence-preservation standard, at the level of *what must be true* of a source.
- The separation of inclusion, privacy and scope judgments.
- The rule that a human decides publication.
- Failure modes the design guards against.
- Honest status: a private workflow in regular use, not an autonomous system.

## Withheld

| Withheld | Reason |
|---|---|
| Subject and candidate names, and rejection reasons | Privacy and dignity of identifiable living people |
| Drafts and article text | Unpublished work about real people |
| Source captures and their metadata | Access terms; internal verification artifacts |
| Credentials, sessions, and how access is obtained | Access terms and security |
| Reviewer identity and per-decision history | Personal working record |
| Agent roles, channel identifiers, routing, hostnames | Operational detail; not needed to judge the design |
| Monitoring state for specific live pages | Reveals the project's current attention |

## Rules applied

1. **No identifiable individuals.** Not "anonymised" — absent. Pseudonyms in an editorial
   context are re-identifiable by anyone who reads the sources.
2. **No text from drafts or articles**, including short quotes used as illustration.
3. **No procedures for access-controlled sources.** The obligation is stated; the method is not.
4. **No absence used as a claim.** A missing entry is simply missing, never "therefore private".
5. **Owner review before publication** of anything describing the editorial work.
6. **Private first.** Any repository for this work is drafted private and made public only after
   content review.

## Why this boundary is the substance of the project

For most portfolios, a privacy section is a courtesy. Here it *is* the design: a system whose
purpose is to write about people must be able to state, plainly and in advance, what it will
never handle — and that statement is more informative to a reader than any implementation
diagram would be.
